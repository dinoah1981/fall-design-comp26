// 01 — the challenge

One week. One problem.
One working app.

Your team will identify a real problem in our community — inside Comp Sci High or out in the Bronx — and build a working app that helps solve it. You'll use AI tools to move at startup speed, and at the end of the week you'll pitch your product to judges like founders pitching investors.

REQ_01

Community Problem

Your app must help solve or address a real problem in the community — your school or the Bronx. Real users, real stakes. If it doesn't help somebody, it doesn't count.

REQ_02

AI-Built MVP

Ship a minimum viable product built with AI tools. Not a mockup, not a slide — working software people can actually touch. Every AI tool you use must be disclosed in your submission.

REQ_03

VC Pitch Video

One 1–3 minute video that pitches your product like you're raising a round — and doubles as your official Congressional App Challenge demo video. One take, two jobs.

→ This competition feeds directly into the 2026 Congressional App Challenge — the official app competition of the U.S. House of Representatives. Top teams' apps will be submitted before the national deadline: Monday, October 26, 2026, 12:00 PM EDT.

// 02 — your team

Four roles. One startup.

Teams of up to four, and every member owns a job. Small teams ship — yours will run like an early-stage startup for one week.

role_01.md

Product Manager

// the operator

Owns the problem statement and the plan. Keeps the team scoped, on schedule, and honest about what ships by Friday.

role_02.md

Product Designer

// the architect

Owns the user experience — who it's for, how it flows, why it's obvious to use. Turns a community problem into screens that make sense.

role_03.md

Lead Engineer

// the builder

Owns the build. Drives the AI tools, wires the pieces together, and makes sure there's a working MVP on demo day — not a demo of excuses.

role_04.md

Pitch Lead

// the closer

Owns the story. Scripts and fronts the VC pitch video, runs pitch practice, and sells the vision to judges on expo day.

// 05 — the rules

Know the rules.
Then play to win.

We run on the official 2026 Congressional App Challenge rulebook, plus three house rules that make this competition ours. The highlights:

eligibility

  • Teams of up to 4 — or compete solo
  • One app per student per year, one district
  • Apps created after Oct 30, 2025 are fair game

the build

  • Any platform, any language
  • Must actually work — functionality counts
  • All AI use must be disclosed — and AI can't be the whole build

the video

  • 1–3 minutes, public on YouTube or Vimeo
  • Names, app name, purpose, audience, tools
  • Show the app actually running

// 06 — deliverables

What you ship.

01

The MVP

A working, AI-built minimum viable product that addresses your community problem. Judges can ask to see it run — and the source behind it.

02

The Pitch Video

One video, two jobs: a VC-style pitch that also satisfies every official demo-video requirement — team names, app name, one-sentence purpose, target audience, tools and languages used, and the app in action. 1–3 minutes, uploaded public to YouTube or Vimeo.

03

The Written Answers

The CAC submission questions: what your app does, what inspired it, the technical challenge you overcame, what you learned, and what a 2.0 would look like.

// 07 — judging & prizes

Demo day.

Friday is the judging expo — 11/12 in the morning, 9/10 before lunch, winners announced by early afternoon. Judges score three things:

/idea

Quality of the Idea

Creativity, originality, and how squarely it hits a real community problem.

/build

Implementation

User experience and design — does the MVP feel like a product, not a project?

/skill

Technical Excellence

Demonstrated coding and programming skill — including how intelligently you drove your AI tools.

What winners get

  • > School winners crowned Friday — by grade band, in front of everyone
  • > Top apps submitted to the 2026 Congressional App Challenge
  • > CAC district winners are featured on House.gov and the official CAC site
  • > District winners qualify for a copyright fee waiver under the ARTS Act
